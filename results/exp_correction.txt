   Where the asymptote starts is itself a measurement. resid*m^1.5 is
   flat at -0.103 from m=100 onward, is 13% off it at m=50, and is 2.6x

   might suggest. That threshold is the operationally useful form of the

   Note that this and the residual test are NOT independent checks of
   beta: both are the first-order expansion of the same shifted closed
   form. The independent evidence is (a) the residual exponent: a beta
   wrong by a relative eps would leave an O(sqrt(dt)) residual, not the
   O(dt^1.5) measured above, and (b) the Spitzer route in exp_overshoot,
   which never looks at an option price at all.

why the one-term log-log slope reads 0.4665 instead of 0.5000:
   because c2*dt is not negligible. With c1 = 8.381 and c2 = -14.130, the
   dt term is 15.1% of the sqrt(dt) term at m=25 and still 1.9% at
   m=1600. Fitting a single power through a two-term expansion returns
   an average of the two exponents, not the leading one.

--- delta: what the shift buys the hedge -----------------------------

      contract        |  m  | ratio |   exact   |  shifted (err)   | uncorrected (err)
----------------------+-----+-------+-----------+------------------+-------------------
headline, daily       |  50 |  2.70 |  0.746772 |  0.746657  -0.02% |  0.797614  +6.81%
headline, 252 fix     | 252 |  6.07 |  0.773972 |  0.773973  +0.00% |  0.797614  +3.05%
barrier 50bp away     | 252 |  0.40 |  1.154489 |  1.333306 +15.49% |  1.390217 +20.42%

    On the headline contract the shift preserves the analytic delta to
    0.015%, which is the production case for it: the fast closed-form
    pricer keeps both its price and its hedge. At a validity ratio of
    0.40 it does not. There the shifted price is still 4.60% off (1.661330
    against 1.741485) and the delta is 15% off, so the correction buys
    almost nothing exactly where the hedge is most dangerous, and the
    contract belongs on a lattice instead.

--- cost -------------------------------------------------------------

   corrected closed form  : 155 ns per price (best of 5)
   convolution benchmark  : 368 ms at m=50

   Do not read that ratio as a lattice-versus-formula benchmark. This
   benchmark is deliberately extravagant: it solves the whole value
   function on two grids and extrapolates, to get six digits it does not
   need for pricing. A production lattice for one price is far cheaper
   than 368 ms. The honest statement is the order of magnitude: a
   closed form is tens of nanoseconds and any grid method is
   milliseconds, so the shift is what decides whether a discretely-fixed
   barrier can live in the pricer that gets called on every quote.
