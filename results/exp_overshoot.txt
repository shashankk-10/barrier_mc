
=== exp_overshoot: recovering beta from Spitzer's identity ============
beta as used by bgk.hpp : 0.58259715793901068
zeta(1/2)               : -1.4603545088095868
-zeta(1/2)/sqrt(2 pi)   : 0.58259715793901079

[1] sum_{k=1..n} k^{-1/2} - 2 sqrt(n)  ->  zeta(1/2)
    and the next term, (sum - 2 sqrt(n) - zeta(1/2)) * 2 sqrt(n) -> 1

          n |  kahan sum - 2sqrt(n) |   naive - kahan |  next-term check
------------+-----------------------+-----------------+------------------
        100 |       -1.410396175216 |       -3.55e-15 |         0.999167
      10000 |       -1.455354550476 |       +3.41e-13 |         0.999992
    1000000 |       -1.459854508851 |       +5.00e-11 |         1.000000
  100000000 |       -1.460304508808 |       +1.85e-09 |         1.000000
      limit |       -1.460354508810 |

[2] E[M_n] = (1/sqrt(2 pi)) sum k^{-1/2}   (Spitzer + Gaussian steps)
    E[M_n] - sqrt(2n/pi)  ->  -beta

          n |     E[M_n] exact  |  E[M_n] - sqrt(2n/pi) |  recovered beta

      10000 |      79.207853617 |       -0.580602463160 |    0.5825971746

  100000000 |    7978.263030818 |       -0.582577210823 |    0.5825971579
      limit |                   |       -0.582597157939 |    0.5825971579

[4] beta from simulation alone, using no identity at all:
    beta_hat = sqrt(2n/pi) - E[M_n]_MC + (1/2)/sqrt(2 pi n)

     n |   beta_hat  |    s.e.   | deviation from beta
-------+-------------+-----------+--------------------
    25 |    0.580272 |  0.001502 | -1.55 s.e.
   100 |    0.577521 |  0.003011 | -1.69 s.e.
   400 |    0.578074 |  0.006026 | -0.75 s.e.

    The standard error grows like sqrt(n) while the finite-n bias
    shrinks like n^{-3/2}, so there is a floor on how well this route can
    do on a laptop. That is precisely why bgk.hpp takes beta from the
    zeta value and this experiment only has to confirm it.
