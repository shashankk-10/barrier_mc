[2] the Gaussian bridge, carried over unchanged, is now biased
    Applying the endpoint formula ignores every crossing caused by a jump
    inside an interval. The jump-aware estimator conditions on the jump
    times and sizes and is exact again; the difference is the bias.

      m |  naive bridge  |  jump-aware   |   bias (s.e.)      | bias * m
--------+----------------+---------------+--------------------+----------
     25 |       4.609262 |      4.538370 | +0.070892 (0.000780) |  +1.7723
     50 |       4.601434 |      4.564544 | +0.036890 (0.000567) |  +1.8445
    100 |       4.594314 |      4.575779 | +0.018535 (0.000401) |  +1.8535
    200 |       4.593437 |      4.584780 | +0.008658 (0.000265) |  +1.7315
    400 |       4.596019 |      4.591687 | +0.004333 (0.000187) |  +1.7331

    bias * m roughly constant means the naive estimator's error is
    O(dt): the chance of a jump inside an interval is lambda*dt, and a
    jump is the only thing the endpoint formula cannot see. Under pure
    diffusion the same column would be identically zero.

[3] the continuity correction under jumps
    'gap' is discrete minus continuous, both from the jump-aware
    estimator on the same paths. 'residual' is the same difference with
    the continuous leg evaluated at the shifted barrier.

    Read the |res|/s.e. column first. At lambda = 0 the true residual is
    about 4e-5 (exp_correction measures it against the convolution
    benchmark, which has no sampling error); that is an order of magnitude
    below what two million paths can resolve, so those rows are noise and
    their 'improvement' figures mean nothing. That is not a failure of the
    experiment. It is the reason the rest of this repo does not use Monte
    Carlo to measure the residual. With jumps the residual rises above the
    noise floor, and then the scaling can be read off.

    THE BOUNDARY, stated as a rate instead of as an adjective:

      pure diffusion   gap = O(dt^0.5),  residual = O(dt^1.5)
      with jumps       gap = O(dt^0.5),  residual = O(dt^1.0)

    The gap column confirms the first half in both cases: a fourfold
    refinement halves it, which is dt^0.5. The res*m column confirms the
    second: it is roughly constant once jumps dominate, so the residual is
    O(dt), whereas exp_correction's res*m^1.5 column is the constant one
    under pure diffusion.

    So the correction keeps working. It still removes most of the gap
    and it is free, but it stops being asymptotically exact. The barrier
    shift compensates for a GAUSSIAN overshoot, and the part of the gap
    caused by a jump crossing the barrier and returning inside a monitoring
    interval is untouched by it. The improvement factor therefore grows
    like sqrt(m) with jumps instead of like m without them.
